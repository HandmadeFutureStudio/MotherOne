Mother
One

300 years after the invention of the Pill, Mother One is a symbol of protecting choice.

Read her backstory and then place a pill to cast your vote.

For centuries, control over conception was seized and weaponized — pregnancy forced, tracked, punished, owed to the state rather than the body carrying it. Every method ever built to stop that — a pill, a device, a law — could still be overridden by force. What if biology itself protected consent to conceive?

In 2016, evolutionary biologists proposed that female orgasm is the last trace of a mechanism still active in cats and other species, where an egg is released by orgasm, not a fixed cycle — dormant in humans, but never gone. An intergenerational collective, working in secret across generations, found a way to reactivate it. They called the treatment the Birthright.

The word was not new — it had long belonged to those who fought hardest against ending a pregnancy, protecting the fetus from the moment of conception onward. The collective didn't discard the word. They moved what it protected earlier, and who it protected: not the state's claim on a body, but the woman's own choice.

Pill 2.0 is what triggers this biology without it, conception cannot happen, by desire or by force. The Pill 2.0 vote was global and decided whether it would be legal.

Mother One is the symbol of what was fought for, bodily autonomy built into the body itself, in order for children to be conceived of consent and desire.

  • 1973

  • 1976
    The Hyde Amendment bars federal funds from covering abortion except in narrow cases.
  • 1992
    Planned Parenthood v. Casey narrows Roe's protections while stopping short of overturning it.
  • 2021
    Texas enacts SB 8, banning abortion after six weeks and deputizing private citizens to enforce it.
  • 2022
    Roe v. Wade is overturned in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization.
  • 2022–25
    Georgia enacts fetal personhood provisions — tax dependent status and child support eligibility for a fetus. Fetal personhood laws then spread rapidly state by state: Alabama's Supreme Court rules frozen embryos are legally children; multiple states pass "Baby Olivia" laws; dozens of personhood bills are introduced nationwide. The federal Life at Conception Act is reintroduced in Congress.
  • 2026
    The first fully personalized, bespoke in vivo CRISPR therapy is designed and delivered to a patient in just six months — proof that gene editing can already work as a single infusion, with no hospitalization required.

Timeline II · Speculative Future History

speculative future history

  • 2026–40s
    State-level personhood laws spread to critical mass, consolidating pressure toward federal action.
  • 2040s–60s
    Digital reproductive surveillance normalizes. Period-tracking data and pharmacy records are used to investigate suspected abortions.
  • 2080s
    The Life at Conception Act finally passes, consolidating the state-by-state patchwork into unified federal law.
  • 2085
    AI-designed gene editors and research copilots descended from tools like CRISPR-GPT and OpenCRISPR-1 succeed in the first fully AI-designed gene-editing enzyme.
  • 2090s–2110s
    Allied regimes coordinate through trade and "family values" pacts. Control over womb bearers becomes explicitly global.
  • 2085–2130s
    The intergenerational collective continues the research in secret, passing knowledge forward across generations.
  • 2140–54
    The Sovereignty Wars. Bodily autonomy is the founding cause; The Birthright treatment becomes its unifying, culminating demand.
  • 2154
    The Birthright treatment and the Pill 2.0 is completed the identy of the first test subject is reffered to as Mother One.
  • 2155
    The war ends. Whether the Birthright treatment and The Pill 2.0 would be legal to choose is put to a planetary vote.
  • 2155
    The collective refuses to patent the Birthright treatment itself, citing Jonas Salk's 1955 refusal to patent the polio vaccine. The core treatment reaches millions within a decade.
  • 2156–70s
    Where the vote passes, the Birthright becomes legally available, chosen individually, body by body — never mandated. Some nations and regions reject it outright, banning it even for individuals who want it, echoing older patchworks of reproductive law.
  • 2170s–90s
    In adopting regions, family structures diversify openly. Surrogacy and donor conception, no longer shadowed by coercion, become common and celebrated paths to parenthood, including for queer couples and solo parents by choice. Communal child-rearing re-emerges alongside traditional households. In rejecting regions, people travel — or go underground — to access what their own government denies them.
  • 2190s–2210s
    Institutions once built around controlling reproduction — marriage as contract, inheritance tied to legitimacy, criminalized pregnancy — are slowly dismantled or reformed. Sex education shifts globally from fear and prohibition toward pleasure and consent.
  • 2210s–30s
    The first generation is born entirely after the Birthright. For them, coerced conception is not a lived fear but a studied historical horror. Art, ritual, and story begin mythologizing the first woman to undergo The Birthright treament as MOTHER ONE.
  • 2260
    Present day of the artwork. Mother One is symbol of protection carved into metal, stone, worn as jewelry, painted as mural, tattooed as marker.

Seed of this Idea

I had this idea when I was fifteen, living in Southern Idaho in a fundamentalist Mormon community. I wasn't yet sexually active, and I hadn't yet come out to myself as queer. But I already understood, clearly, the danger of patriarchal control over conception — how much power was placed in the hands of men, and how little was left in the hands of the body carrying a possible pregnancy.

I wrote an early version of this idea as a creative writing assignment for my English teacher. I didn't have the language yet for bodily autonomy, and I imagined a way biology itself could be a solution. I got in trouble for writing the story as vulgar.

Returning to this idea now with speculative design as the context has been a meaningful exploration.
